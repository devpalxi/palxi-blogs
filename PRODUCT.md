# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Australians aged 50 and over with no technical background: marina and caravan-park managers, pub owners, small-business operators, and the customers who pay them. They read on an iPad at the kitchen table or on a phone in reading glasses, often in the afternoon, usually unhurried. Many have been targeted by scams (Australians 65+ carried 26.5% of reported scam losses in 2025 while being 17.1% of the population, per the ACCC), so they arrive cautious. The job: understand, in plain words, whether Palxi's products (harbr, Cruz, and NT Government systems) are safe to trust with their money and data.

## Product Purpose

The Palxi blog explains how Palxi designs and builds fintech products, so non-technical readers come away understanding the idea and trusting the company. Each post takes one technical idea (payment flows, prototyping, feature branches, compliance, design systems) and makes it clear through plain language, labelled diagrams, and gentle animation. Success is a reader who finishes the post, could explain the idea to a friend, and feels calmer about using Palxi's products.

## Brand Personality

Calm, clear, dependable. The voice of a trusted local bank manager explaining something over a cup of tea: patient, specific, never condescending, never salesy. Australian English throughout. Confidence comes from showing exactly how things work, not from big claims.

## Anti-references

- Crypto and trading apps: dark neon dashboards, charts everywhere, hype energy.
- Big-bank corporate: navy-and-gold, stock photos of handshakes, legal-sounding copy.
- Startup SaaS templates: gradient heroes, identical icon-card grids, buzzwords ("seamless", "elevate", "next-gen").
- Government form sites: dense, grey, bureaucratic, small text.

## Design Principles

1. **Show, then tell.** Every technical idea gets a picture: a labelled diagram, a generic wireframe, or an animation that walks through it. Text supports the picture, not the other way round.
2. **Plain words first.** Define any unavoidable term the first time it appears. If a sentence needs a second read, rewrite it.
3. **Practise what we preach.** A post about trustworthy design must itself be calm, honest, and easy to read. No tricks, no clutter, no hidden anything.
4. **Respect the reader's pace.** Nothing moves unless it helps understanding, nothing auto-plays past the reader, and every animation can be replayed or switched off.
5. **Company-wide, never product-specific.** Posts explain how Palxi works across all its products. No worked examples from a single product, no invented businesses, names, amounts or reference numbers. Products may be named once in passing, never used as the story.
6. **Specific over impressive.** Real Australian context (PayTo, Confirmation of Payee, Scamwatch) and cited facts beat vague claims and invented statistics.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA as the floor, tuned for older readers:

- Body text around 19-20px, never below 16px anywhere (including captions and labels), line-height around 1.65, lines capped near 68 characters.
- Text contrast at least 4.5:1, and in practice aim for 6:1 or more for body copy.
- Tap targets at least 44 by 44px; visible focus rings on everything interactive.
- All motion respects `prefers-reduced-motion`: diagrams show their finished state instantly, no movement.
- Diagrams carry text labels, not colour alone, and have a text alternative or a caption that explains them.
- Images carry descriptive alt text and visible credit lines for open-licence sources.
