# ISO 27001 certification in Australia: cost and timeline

Picture a supplier form from a bank. You run a small finance business, and the bank wants to use your product. On page three, one question stops you: "Are you ISO 27001 certified?"

If the answer is no, two questions follow fast. What does ISO 27001 certification in Australia cost, and how long does it take? This guide answers both in plain words. It also explains what the auditor checks, and why the bank asked in the first place.

The dollar figures and timings below are planning estimates for a typical small business. The assumptions are spelled out. Your own quotes will differ.

## What ISO 27001 certification is, in plain words

ISO 27001 is an international standard for keeping business information safe. ISO, the body that publishes it, calls it "the world's best-known standard for information security management systems" ([ISO](https://www.iso.org/standard/27001)).

That phrase sounds grand. An information security management system is simply the rules and daily habits a business uses to protect its information. Think of it as the house rules for your data, plus proof that people follow them.

The current edition came out in 2022. The standard itself is short: 19 pages, sold by ISO for 155 Swiss francs ([ISO](https://www.iso.org/standard/27001)). Most of the work sits in a list of 93 security controls, sorted into four groups ([IAF](https://iaf.nu/iaf_system/uploads/documents/IAF_MD_26_Transition_requirements_for_ISOIEC_27001-2022_09082022.pdf), August 2022). A control is one specific safeguard. Locking lost laptops remotely, checking new staff and keeping backups are all examples.

You don't have to use every control. You choose the ones that suit your risks. Then you write a list called a Statement of Applicability, which shows each control and whether you use it. The International Accreditation Forum (IAF), a global group of accreditation bodies, says this comparison helps make sure nothing needed is "inadvertently omitted".

The checking is done by an independent audit firm, called a certification body. ISO says a certificate from an accredited firm "may bring an additional layer of confidence" ([ISO](https://www.iso.org/standard/27001)). Accredited means another independent body has confirmed the audit firm knows its job.

If a supplier shows you an older certificate, check the edition. Certificates to the 2013 version had to expire or be withdrawn when a three-year switchover period ended ([IAF](https://iaf.nu/iaf_system/uploads/documents/IAF_MD_26_Transition_requirements_for_ISOIEC_27001-2022_09082022.pdf)).

## Why Australian fintechs get asked for it

The question comes from the customer's side. Banks, insurers and super funds are overseen by the Australian Prudential Regulation Authority (APRA). Its security standard, CPS 234, makes them check anyone who handles their information. Where a third party manages that information, the regulated firm "must assess the information security capability of that party" ([APRA, CPS 234](https://www.apra.gov.au/standards/cps-234)).

CPS 234 binds the bank, not its suppliers. It doesn't mention ISO 27001 at all. Still, the bank must gather evidence about you somehow, and an independent certificate is one tidy way to supply part of it. A bank may still ask for more, such as test results or a long questionnaire. The thinking behind those forms is clearer once you see [the security questions a board should ask under CPS 234](/blog/apra-cps-234-board-questions).

Firms with an Australian financial services licence carry their own duties. In February 2026 the Federal Court ordered FIIG Securities, a firm that deals in bonds, to pay a $2.5 million penalty. The case was brought by the Australian Securities and Investments Commission (ASIC). FIIG notified some 18,000 clients that their personal information may have been compromised. ASIC says it is the first time the court has imposed civil penalties for cyber security failures under the general duties of a licence holder.

The case was about basic safeguards, not about ISO 27001. ASIC's list of failures included multi-factor authentication for remote users (a second login check, such as a code sent to a phone). It also included regular security testing and an incident plan tested every year. ASIC Deputy Chair Sarah Court was blunt. She said the consequences "far exceeded what it would have cost FIIG to implement adequate controls in the first place" ([ASIC](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2026-releases/26-021mr-asic-action-sees-fiig-securities-ordered-to-pay-25-million-over-cyber-security-failures), February 2026).

Privacy law matters too. Businesses covered by the Privacy Act must take reasonable steps to protect personal information. The privacy regulator, the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (OAIC), says those steps include "technical and organisational measures" ([OAIC](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-11-app-11-security-of-personal-information)).

Breaches are not rare. The OAIC received 1,205 data breach notifications in 2025, up 8% on 2024. Financial services made 157 of them, second only to health ([OAIC](https://www.oaic.gov.au/news/media-centre/data-breach-notifications-increase-to-all-time-high-in-2025,-new-ndb-stats-show), July 2026).

You may also be asked for a SOC 2 report, an American style of security audit report. It works differently, and [SOC 2 explained for buyers](/blog/soc-2-for-buyers) walks through what those reports contain.

## What ISO 27001 certification in Australia costs

![A few Australian coins, including a 20 cent piece, resting on a banknote](australian-coins-notes.jpg)

*Australian coins resting on a banknote.*

There is no set price. Audit firms that offer ISO 27001 Australia-wide quote job by job. The figures below are planning estimates. They assume a hypothetical business that has:

- about 30 people, counting staff and contractors
- one office and one product, run in the cloud
- some good habits already, such as backups and login codes, but few written rules
- nobody working on security full time

| Cost item | What you are paying for | Planning range (ex GST) |
|---|---|---|
| The standard | A copy of ISO 27001 from ISO | 155 Swiss francs |
| Setup help | An advisor who helps you assess risks and write the rules | $15,000 to $50,000 |
| Compliance software (optional) | A tool that tracks tasks and stores records | $10,000 to $30,000 a year |
| Penetration test | Hired testers try to break in, then report what they found | $13,500 to $45,000 |
| Certification audit | The audit firm's stage 1 and stage 2 visits | $12,000 to $30,000 |
| Yearly check-ups | A smaller audit in each of the next two years | $6,000 to $15,000 a year |

Add up the first year and outside costs land somewhere between about $40,000 and $155,000. That is a wide range. It narrows once you have two or three real quotes.

Audit time, and so the audit fee, is worked out from headcount. Audit firms count everyone working in the area being certified, "regardless of whether they are members of the organization or not" ([usd AG](https://www.usd.de/en/news-new-iso-iec-27006-requirements/)). So contractors count too. Under the 2024 rules, the number of office sites no longer sets the audit time.

One cost never shows up on an invoice: your own team's time. Someone has to own the work. For the business above, plan on one person spending a day or two a week on it for six to nine months. Managers will need a few hours each month as well.

A penetration test is a planned, authorised attack by hired testers who then write up what they found. It sits in the table because buyers in finance may ask for recent results. The range matches the scope assumptions in our piece on [penetration testing for financial platforms](/blog/penetration-testing-financial-platforms).

## How long does it take to get ISO 27001 certified?

![A white desk clock beside a pen, a notebook and a tablet on a white desk](clock-on-desk.jpg)

*A desk clock, a pen and a notebook.*

For the same hypothetical business, plan on six to twelve months. A business that already runs tidy, written processes can move faster. One that starts from nothing, or changes its product halfway, will take longer.

| Stage | What happens | Rough time |
|---|---|---|
| Gap check | Compare what you do today with what the standard asks | 2 to 4 weeks |
| Set up | Assess risks, write the rules, fix the gaps | 3 to 6 months |
| Run and record | Let the controls run, keep records, hold an internal audit and a management review | 2 to 3 months |
| Stage 1 audit | The auditor reads your documents and checks you are ready | 1 to 2 days, then a few weeks to fix issues |
| Stage 2 audit | The auditor checks the system works in practice | A few days |
| Decision | The audit firm decides and issues the certificate | A few weeks |

It can be tempting to rush the run and record stage. The auditor will look for it. Under the international rules audit firms follow, stage 1 checks whether "internal audits and management reviews are being planned and performed". Stage 2 then checks how well the system works, including those reviews ([IAS](https://www.iasonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/17021-1-2015-Section-9.pdf)).

Those two terms are simpler than they sound. An internal audit is your own check that people follow the rules. A management review is a meeting where leaders look at the results and decide what to change.

So you need some months of real records before stage 2. Who was given access, and when. Which laptops were updated. Which risks were reviewed, and by whom. Which suppliers were checked. Records like these build up slowly, month by month.

Plan around busy periods. A product launch in the middle of the run and record stage will squeeze both. For what happens in the early weeks, see our guide to [ISO 27001 gap analysis and implementation](/blog/iso-27001-gap-analysis-implementation).

## The certificate runs on a three-year cycle

Getting certified is a start, and the costs carry on. The first three-year cycle begins on the day the audit firm makes its decision. Within 12 months, the auditor comes back for a surveillance audit, a smaller yearly check-up. A second one follows in year two. Then comes a full recertification audit in year three, before the certificate runs out ([IAS](https://www.iasonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/17021-1-2015-Section-9.pdf)).

Those visits only go well if the controls keep running between them. We maintain ISO 27001 controls on a client platform that is under external audit. In practice that means steady, ordinary tasks, such as reviewing who has access and keeping software and records up to date.

Budget for the yearly audit fee and for those hours. If you add a product, move offices or bring on a big new supplier, tell your audit firm early. The scope of the certificate may need to change.

## Ways to keep the bill down

Set the scope with care. The scope is the part of your business the certificate covers. A narrower scope can mean fewer people in the count, and so fewer audit days. It still has to cover what your customers care about, which is usually the product they buy and the team that runs it.

Build the rules into daily work. A rule that lives inside the tools people already use costs less to prove than a rule kept in a binder. Login codes switched on for every account are a good example.

Get quotes from two or three accredited audit firms. Ask each one how many audit days it plans, and why.

Use the work twice. The same records can help with a SOC 2 report or the next customer questionnaire.

Don't buy software before you know what it is for. A small team can start with a shared folder and a simple register of risks, then decide later.

## Common questions

### Is ISO 27001 certification required by law in Australia?

The rules covered here don't require it. APRA's CPS 234 doesn't name it, and the privacy principles ask for reasonable steps rather than one particular standard. It becomes a must when a customer's contract says so. Check your own position with your legal advisor.

### Is "ISO 27001 aligned" the same as certified?

No. "Aligned" means a business says it follows the standard, but no outside auditor has checked. ISO suggests quoting the full reference, for example "certified to ISO/IEC 27001:2022" ([ISO](https://www.iso.org/standard/27001)). If a supplier says "aligned", ask what an independent party has actually tested.

### How can I check that a supplier really holds a certificate?

Ask for a copy. Look at the name of the audit firm and whether it is accredited. Then read the scope and the expiry date. A certificate that covers one office or one product may not cover the service you are buying. More checks worth making are listed in [technical due diligence on a build team](/blog/technical-due-diligence-build-team).

## What to do next

Get the facts in hand before you ask for quotes:

- Decide which product and team the certificate needs to cover.
- Count everyone in that area, staff and contractors.
- Run a short gap check against the standard.
- Name one person to own the work, and free up their time.
- Ask two or three accredited audit firms for quotes.
