# ISO 27001 certification in Australia: cost and timeline

Picture this common scenario: you run a growing Australian financial technology firm, specialized lending business, or regional advisory service. A major Australian retail bank or large industry superannuation fund wants to partner with you. After weeks of positive discussions, their procurement team emails over a twenty-page supplier questionnaire. On page three, one straightforward question brings everything to a halt: *"Does your organisation hold independent ISO 27001 certification?"*

If your current answer is no, two practical questions follow immediately: how much will ISO 27001 certification cost in Australia, and how many months will it take to achieve?

Many Australian business founders, directors, and executives find international security standards intimidating. The technical jargon makes the process sound like rocket science. In truth, ISO 27001 is simply an independent, audited proof of good business housekeeping. Here is a clear, plain-language breakdown of what the certification entails, realistic Australian cost estimates, how long the audit takes, and practical ways to keep the consulting bill down.

## What ISO 27001 certification is, in plain words

ISO 27001 is an internationally recognised set of standards designed to help businesses protect their confidential records and computer systems. The International Organization for Standardization (ISO) describes it as the world's most trusted benchmark for an "information security management system" ([ISO](https://www.iso.org/standard/27001)).

While that sounds grand, an "information security management system" is simply the collection of everyday rules, software settings, and staff habits a business relies on to keep customer data safe. Think of it as the clear house rules for your company's digital information, alongside documented proof that your team follows those rules every single day.

The current global standard was published in 2022. The formal standard itself is concise—just 19 pages long, sold by ISO for 155 Swiss francs ([ISO](https://www.iso.org/standard/27001)). Most of the practical work centers around a catalog of 93 specific security controls divided into four categories: organizational rules, people checks, physical office security, and technology protections ([IAF](https://iaf.nu/iaf_system/uploads/documents/IAF_MD_26_Transition_requirements_for_ISOIEC_27001-2022_09082022.pdf), August 2022). A "control" is simply a concrete safeguard—such as locking lost laptops remotely, running police background checks on new staff, or maintaining isolated backups.

You are not required to adopt all 93 controls blindly. Instead, your leadership team assesses your business risks and selects the safeguards that make sense for your operations. You document this selection in a formal document called a "Statement of Applicability". The International Accreditation Forum (IAF) explains that this formal comparison ensures no essential security protection is inadvertently overlooked.

The actual evaluation is conducted by an independent auditing company known as an accredited certification body. "Accredited" means an independent government or international authority has inspected the audit firm and confirmed they possess genuine expertise.

If a potential supplier shows you an older ISO certificate, check the edition date carefully. Certificates issued under the superseded 2013 version expired following a mandatory three-year transition window ([IAF](https://iaf.nu/iaf_system/uploads/documents/IAF_MD_26_Transition_requirements_for_ISOIEC_27001-2022_09082022.pdf)).

## Why Australian fintechs get asked for it

The pressure to achieve ISO 27001 comes almost entirely from your customers and commercial partners. Australian retail banks, general insurers, and superannuation trustees are heavily regulated by the Australian Prudential Regulation Authority (APRA). APRA's mandatory cyber security standard, Prudential Standard CPS 234, requires regulated institutions to thoroughly examine any external technology supplier handling sensitive records ([APRA, CPS 234](https://www.apra.gov.au/standards/cps-234)).

While CPS 234 legally binds the bank rather than you, the bank must collect verifiable proof of your safeguards. An accredited ISO 27001 certificate is the cleanest, most widely accepted way to satisfy the bank's risk committee. To understand what bank directors look for, explore our companion guide on [the cyber security questions a board should ask under CPS 234](/blog/apra-cps-234-board-questions).

Australian financial firms also face direct scrutiny from the Australian Securities and Investments Commission (ASIC). In February 2026, the Federal Court ordered Australian bond broker FIIG Securities to pay a landmark **$2.5 million AUD civil penalty** following an ASIC lawsuit. FIIG had been forced to notify approximately 18,000 clients that their personal information may have been compromised by hackers. ASIC confirmed this was the first time an Australian court imposed severe financial penalties for cyber security failings under the general licensing duties of an Australian Financial Services Licence (AFSL).

Significantly, the court case focused on everyday operational lapses rather than arcane technical flaws. FIIG failed to enforce multi-factor authentication (a secondary login verification code sent to a mobile phone) across remote staff logins, failed to conduct regular penetration testing, and lacked a tested cyber incident plan. ASIC Deputy Chair Sarah Court remarked bluntly that the legal consequences and reputational damage "far exceeded what it would have cost to implement adequate controls in the first place" ([ASIC](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2026-releases/26-021mr-asic-action-sees-fiig-securities-ordered-to-pay-25-million-over-cyber-security-failures), February 2026).

Australian privacy laws add further statutory weight. The Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (OAIC) requires all organisations governed by the *Privacy Act 1988* to take reasonable technical and organizational steps to protect personal records ([OAIC](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-11-app-11-security-of-personal-information)).

Cyber breaches are now frequent occurrences. The OAIC recorded 1,205 data breach notifications during 2025, with financial services accounting for 157 major breaches—the second highest sector in the country ([OAIC](https://www.oaic.gov.au/news/media-centre/data-breach-notifications-increase-to-all-time-high-in-2025,-new-ndb-stats-show), July 2026).

If you sell software into North American markets, enterprise buyers may ask for a SOC 2 report instead. Our breakdown of [SOC 2 compliance for buyers and vendors](/blog/soc-2-for-buyers) explains how SOC 2 differs from ISO 27001.

## What ISO 27001 certification in Australia costs

![A few Australian coins, including a 20 cent piece, resting on a banknote](australian-coins-notes.jpg)

*Planning realistic budgets for ISO 27001 prevents unexpected consulting surprises as your business scales.*

There is no fixed statutory price for ISO 27001 certification in Australia. Accredited audit firms quote each engagement individually. To provide practical guidance, the planning estimates below reflect a typical small-to-medium Australian financial business with:

- Approximately 30 people (combining permanent employees and ongoing contractors)
- A single corporate office with core software hosted in the cloud
- Sensible daily habits already in place (such as daily backups and password managers), but few formal written policies
- No full-time internal cyber security manager on staff

| Investment item | What you are paying for | Realistic planning range (ex GST) |
|---|---|---|
| **The official standard** | Official digital copy of ISO/IEC 27001:2022 from ISO | ~155 Swiss francs (~$270 AUD) |
| **Specialist advisory support** | An external consultant to help assess risks and draft policies | $15,000 to $50,000 AUD |
| **Compliance software (optional)** | Cloud software to track compliance tasks and automate evidence gathering | $10,000 to $30,000 AUD per year |
| **Independent penetration test** | Ethical hackers testing your software for exploitable flaws | $13,500 to $45,000 AUD |
| **Certification audit fee** | Accredited audit firm's Stage 1 and Stage 2 assessment days | $12,000 to $30,000 AUD |
| **Annual surveillance audits** | Lighter annual check-ups conducted in years two and three | $6,000 to $15,000 AUD per year |

Adding these initial items together, total first-year external expenditure generally lands between **$40,000 AUD and $155,000 AUD (excluding GST)**. This range narrows significantly once you request two or three written quotes based on your exact staff numbers.

Audit fees are determined largely by headcount. Accredited auditors count every individual working within the certified business unit, including full-time contractors ([usd AG](https://www.usd.de/en/news-new-iso-iec-27006-requirements/)). Under revised international audit rules, physical office locations no longer inflate audit day counts for cloud-based companies.

The biggest hidden investment is your internal staff time. Achieving certification requires a designated internal champion. For a thirty-person firm, expect one senior operations or technical team member to spend one to two days per week on certification preparations over a six-to-nine-month period.

A penetration test is included in the budget table because institutional banking clients almost universally demand recent independent test reports alongside your certificate. ## How long does it take to get ISO 27001 certified?

![A white desk clock beside a pen, a notebook and a tablet on a white desk](clock-on-desk.jpg)

*Achieving ISO 27001 certification typically requires six to twelve months of disciplined preparation and evidence gathering.*

For a typical mid-sized Australian business, plan on a realistic timeline of **six to twelve months**. An organisation that already operates disciplined, written processes can move faster, whereas a team starting from scratch or undergoing a major product overhaul will require the full year.

| Project stage | Operational focus | Typical timeframe |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Gap analysis** | Compare existing business practices against the 93 ISO controls | 2 to 4 weeks |
| **2. Policy setup & remediation** | Perform formal risk assessments, draft policies, and fix technical gaps | 3 to 6 months |
| **3. Operational evidence gathering** | Run the controls in daily work, conduct internal audits and management reviews | 2 to 3 months |
| **4. Stage 1 audit (readiness review)** | Auditor reviews documentation and confirms readiness for live audit | 1 to 2 days on-site/remote |
| **5. Stage 2 audit (effectiveness test)** | Auditor inspects live systems, samples records, and interviews team members | 3 to 5 audit days |
| **6. Certification decision** | Audit committee reviews findings and formally issues the certificate | 2 to 4 weeks |

It is tempting for eager businesses to try to rush directly from writing policies into the final audit. However, independent auditors will refuse to issue a certificate without evidence of live operation. Under international auditing guidelines, Stage 1 confirms that your organisation has planned and performed internal audits and formal management review meetings, while Stage 2 inspects months of accumulated operational evidence ([IAS](https://www.iasonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/17021-1-2015-Section-9.pdf)).

An "internal audit" simply means an internal review checking whether staff actually follow your policies. A "management review" is an executive meeting where directors evaluate security results and agree on necessary improvements.

You need two to three months of genuine, unalterable operating logs before Stage 2: records showing when staff accounts were created and revoked, logs proving laptops received software updates, documented supplier reviews, and risk registers. These records cannot be hastily fabricated the night before an auditor arrives.

Avoid scheduling your Stage 2 audit during peak commercial periods, such as a major core banking migration or end-of-financial-year reporting. For practical steps on the initial phase, see our guide to [ISO 27001 gap analysis and implementation](/blog/iso-27001-gap-analysis-implementation).

## The certificate runs on a three-year cycle

Receiving your initial ISO 27001 certificate is a major milestone, but compliance is an ongoing operational commitment. The official certificate is valid for three years. Within twelve months of issuance, the certification body returns for a smaller "surveillance audit" to verify that controls remain active. A second surveillance visit occurs in year two, followed by a comprehensive recertification audit in year three before the certificate expires ([IAS](https://www.iasonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/17021-1-2015-Section-9.pdf)).

Surveillance audits run smoothly only when security practices are embedded into everyday routines. At Palxi, we maintain continuous ISO 27001 controls across our financial platforms. In everyday practice, this means maintaining consistent, unglamorous habits: promptly revoking access for departing contractors, keeping software libraries patched, and reviewing vendor agreements annually.

Ensure you budget for annual surveillance audit fees and ongoing maintenance time. If your company acquires a new business or relocates primary data infrastructure, inform your certification body early to adjust your certified scope.

## Ways to keep the bill down

- **Define your audit scope with precision.** The scope defines the exact boundaries of your business covered by the certificate. A carefully defined scope covering only your core digital platform and the engineering team running it results in fewer audited staff days and lower audit invoices.
- **Embed security rules into everyday tools.** Security safeguards built directly into your software—such as mandatory multi-factor authentication enforced automatically across all staff logins—cost far less to audit than paper policies kept in loose-leaf office binders.
- **Solicit competing quotes from accredited auditors.** Approach two or three accredited certification bodies. Ask each firm to explain their allocated audit days and daily assessor rates.
- **Re-use your audit evidence.** The documentary evidence, incident registers, and access logs you compile for ISO 27001 can be re-used directly for institutional customer questionnaires and SOC 2 audits.
- **Avoid expensive compliance software until needed.** A growing business can successfully achieve its initial certification using structured cloud folders, clear registers, and disciplined habits, adopting specialized automated compliance software later as headcount expands.

## Common questions

### Is ISO 27001 certification mandatory by law in Australia?

No. Australian law does not explicitly require ISO 27001 certification. APRA's CPS 234 and the federal Privacy Act focus on outcomes rather than mandating specific badges. However, achieving certification becomes an absolute commercial necessity when major banking partners, institutional investors, or corporate clients make it a condition of contract.

### What is the difference between being "ISO 27001 aligned" and "certified"?

"Aligned" simply means a business claims it adheres to the standard's guidelines, without any independent verification. "Certified" means an accredited, independent auditing firm spent days inspecting evidence, interviewing staff, and verifying controls before formally issuing an accredited certificate. If a technology supplier claims to be "aligned", ask to see the independent audit evidence backing that claim.

### How can our team verify an external supplier's certificate?

Request a full copy of the certificate. Inspect the issuing audit firm's credentials to confirm they are accredited by JAS-ANZ (the Joint Accreditation System of Australia and New Zealand) or an equivalent international accreditation body. Examine the exact "scope of certification" to ensure it covers the specific software product and hosting infrastructure your business relies on. ## Practical next steps for your business

Before reaching out to certification auditors for quotes:

- Clearly identify the specific product, customer data flows, and team members to be certified.
- Complete a precise headcount of all staff and contractors working within that scope.
- Conduct a preliminary gap check against the 93 ISO 27001 controls.
- Nominate a senior internal project lead and allocate protected time in their calendar.
- Request written audit proposals from two or three accredited Australian certification bodies.
